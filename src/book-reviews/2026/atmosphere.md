One of my favourite TV shows is *For All Mankind*, which my partner and I jokingly refer to as the "things go wrong in space show", so she recommended this book to me after about three chapters.
I'm very glad she did.

The story follows Joan, an astronomer who starts training for the Space Shuttle in the early 1980s.
Initially she's only focused on her training and her niece Frances, and doesn't think of herself as somebody romantic -- until she meets fellow astronaut candidate Vanessa.
The two begin seeing each other in secret, in a career and political climate that absolutely will not tolerate any form of "sexual deviance".

Their burgeoning romance is told in parallel with a disaster on the Space Shuttle, in which Vanessa is the only person left concious, and Joan is in the CAPCOM chair.
It's clear that *something* has happened between them, but it takes most of the book for us to learn that Joan tried to break off their relationship shortly before Vanessa's launch, in a misguided attempt to protect Vanessa's flying career.

I'm always a sucker for sapphic romances, and this was no exception.
I enjoy them both realising they can feel this way about anybody (let alone each other), and navigating the conflict with their other desires.
Joan wants to be a surrogate parent to Frances; Vanessa wants to fly the Space Shuttle; they both have to decide how to mesh that with the relationship.

It's a sweet story that blends two of my favourite genres, and I can imagine it being a comfort re-read in future.
